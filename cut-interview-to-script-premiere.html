How to cut an interview to a client's script in Premiere

The client has read the transcript and sent back a script: the lines they want, in the order they want them. Your job is to find each line in hours of interview and lay the soundbites out in that order. Here are three ways to do it in Premiere.

Before any of them: transcribe

All three ways lean on Premiere's transcript. Open the Text panel (Window > Text), and transcribe the clips or the sequence. On a long shoot this takes a while, so start it first.

If you shot with more than one camera and a separate recorder, sync them into one sequence before you cut. Otherwise every soundbite you pull has to be re-synced by hand later.

1. By hand, with the transcript's search

Take the first line of the script, pick three or four distinctive words from it, and search the transcript for them. Click the result to move the playhead there, mark an in and an out in the timeline, and copy that stretch into a new sequence. Then the next line.

This works, and it is how most editors do it. It is slow for two reasons. Clients tidy up what people said, so the script's wording rarely matches the transcript word for word and a search for the whole sentence finds nothing. And the lines are in script order, not the order they were said, so you are jumping all over the interview.

2. With Premiere's Paper Edit

Premiere 26.5 added Paper Edit to the Text panel. You read through the transcript, select the sentences or parts of sentences you want, and press Create new sequence. Premiere builds a sequence from your selections.

It saves the copying and pasting. Finding the lines is still yours to do: Paper Edit assembles what you select, it does not look for a script's lines for you.

3. With Pullquote

Pullquote is a free panel for Premiere that starts from the other end. Paste the client's script into it, exactly as they sent it. It finds where each line was said, even when the wording was tidied, and ignores the names, timecodes and notes clients leave in.

Lines it found turn green. Lines it is less sure of turn amber, with a percentage, so you know which to check. A line nobody said stays plain. Click any word to jump the playhead to it. If a line was said more than once, you can step through the takes.

Then press Build Sequence. You get a new sequence of the soundbites in script order, with every camera and the recorder still in sync. Your original sequence is not changed.

Which to use

For two or three lines, searching by hand is fine. When you are choosing the lines yourself, Paper Edit is the tool. When someone has handed you a script, Pullquote does the finding and the assembling in one go.
