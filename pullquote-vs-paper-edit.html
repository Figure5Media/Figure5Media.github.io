Pullquote and Premiere's Paper Edit: what each one does

Both turn a transcript into a rough cut inside Premiere, and both leave your original sequence alone. They start from different places, and that decides which one you want.

Paper Edit: you choose from the transcript

Paper Edit is part of Premiere's Text panel, added in version 26.5. You select sentences or parts of sentences anywhere in the transcript. The panel shows how many you have selected and how long they run, lets you preview them, and creates a new sequence from them.

It is the right tool when you are the one deciding what stays, reading through and marking the good parts.

Pullquote: you start from a script

Pullquote is a free panel from Figure 5. You paste in a script, and it finds where each line was said in the transcript Premiere made of your sequence. It copes with reworded lines, with lines said in a different order, and with the names, timecodes and editor's marks that come in a client's document.

It shows what it found in green, what to double-check in amber, and what was never said. One press builds a new sequence of those soundbites in script order.

It is the right tool when someone else has already chosen, and handed you the lines in writing.

They work together

Nothing stops you using both. Pullquote to get the scripted lines down in order, then Paper Edit or the timeline for the lines you add yourself. Pullquote reads the transcript Premiere makes, so it needs the clips transcribed first, just as Paper Edit does.
