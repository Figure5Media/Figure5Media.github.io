# How to pull selects from a transcript in Premiere

Pulling selects is the first pass on any interview: getting from an hour of talking to the ten minutes worth keeping. Premiere's transcript makes it far quicker than scrubbing. Here is how each of its tools fits, and what to do when the selects arrive as a document.

## Get a transcript first

Open the Text panel (Window > Text) and transcribe your clips, or the sequence they are in. Fix speaker names if it helps you read; nothing below depends on them.

## Reading and choosing: text-based editing

With a source clip's transcript open, highlight the words you want and use Insert or Overwrite to drop that stretch into the timeline. It is the same three-point edit you already know, driven from the text. It suits working through an interview from top to bottom.

## Choosing many at once: Paper Edit

In Premiere 26.5 and later, the Text panel's Paper Edit lets you select passages from anywhere in the transcript, see how long they run together, preview them, and create a new sequence from them in one step. It suits the pass where you are deciding what is good.

## When the selects come as a list: Pullquote

Often the choosing is not yours. A producer or client reads the transcript and sends back the lines they want, in a document, usually reworded a little and peppered with names and timecodes.

Pullquote, a free panel for Premiere, takes that document as it is. Paste it in and it finds each line in your sequence's transcript, marks what it found and what needs checking, and builds a new sequence of those soundbites in the document's order, with every camera angle and the recorder in sync.

You can still shape it before building: leave a soundbite out, cut words from the middle of one, choose a different take, or drag the lines into a new order.

## Keeping sync

If the interview was shot multicam, pull selects from the synced sequence, not from the single clips. Selects cut from one camera have to be matched to the others afterwards; selects cut from a synced stack come out with every angle in place.
